    Umi‑Time Technical Specification

    Version 1.0 — Planetary Time Standard

    1. Overview

      Umi‑time is a planetary time system that expresses the day as a uniform 0°–360° cycle mapped directly from UTC.
      The dial orientation is solar‑based, but the degree progression is universal and constant.

      Civil time works well for local scheduling, but becomes fragmented and error‑prone across multiple time zones.
      Umi‑time provides a universal timestamp that is the same everywhere on Earth, making global coordination simple and unambiguous.

      This specification defines:

      • the Umi‑time degree system
      • the solar geometry inputs
      • the computational model
      • the dial orientation rules
      • the Umi‑seconds fractional system
      • the display conventions
      • the reference implementation structure

      Umi‑time is an open standard. Anyone may implement it.

    2. Definitions

      1. Solar Day

        The solar day is the interval between two successive solar noons at a given longitude.
        Its length varies slightly throughout the year due to orbital eccentricity and axial tilt.

        Umi‑time does not adjust its degree progression based on the varying length of the solar day.
        Solar‑day variation affects sunrise and sunset times slightly, but does not affect the placement of solar noon or solar midnight on the dial.

      2. Umi‑Time Degrees

        Umi‑time degrees are a continuous measure of daily progress mapped directly from UTC time.
        They range from 0° to 360°, advancing uniformly over the 24‑hour civil day.

        Umi‑time degrees do not represent true solar rotational speed and do not vary with the length of the solar day.
        They provide a universal, constant measure of the day, while the dial orientation maps those degrees to the local sky.

      3. Solar Noon

        The moment when the Sun reaches its highest altitude above the horizon at a given location.

      4. Solar Midnight

        The moment when the Sun reaches its lowest altitude below the horizon at a given location.

      5. Sunrise / Sunset

        The moments when the Sun’s upper limb crosses the horizon, corrected for atmospheric refraction.

      6. Longitude Anchor

        Umi‑time degrees are anchored to the observer’s longitude.
        Longitude determines the alignment of solar noon, solar midnight, and the rotation of the Umi‑time dial.

    3. Core Concept

      Umi‑time represents the day as a full 360° rotational cycle mapped uniformly across the 24‑hour civil day.
      Each degree corresponds to: 

      1/360 of a 24‑hour civil day

      This uniform mapping ensures that Umi‑time degrees advance at a constant rate, remain intuitive, and pair cleanly with civil time.

      Solar geometry determines the orientation of the dial — solar noon at the top, solar midnight at the bottom,
      and sunrise/sunset placed according to their true positions — but does not affect the degree progression.

      Umi‑time degrees are universal; the dial orientation is local.

    4. Solar Geometry Inputs

      Umi‑time requires the following astronomical inputs:

      • Latitude (φ) — Observer’s north–south position on Earth.
      • Longitude (λ) — Observer’s east–west position; determines solar noon/midnight alignment.
      • Solar declination (δ) — Sun’s angular position north or south of the celestial equator.
      • Equation of time (EoT) — Difference between apparent solar time and mean solar time.
      • Solar hour angle (H) — Angular distance of the Sun east or west of local solar noon.
      • Solar altitude (α) — Angle of the Sun above the horizon.
      • Solar azimuth (A) — Compass direction of the Sun along the horizon.
      • Julian date (JD) — Continuous astronomical day count used for solar calculations.

      These values may be obtained from standard solar position algorithms such as:

      • NOAA Solar Calculator
      • NREL SPA (Solar Position Algorithm)
      • Meeus Astronomical Algorithms

    5. Computing Umi‑Time Degrees

      Umi‑time uses a direct, uniform mapping from UTC time to a 0°–360° rotational cycle.
      This ensures that Umi‑time advances at a constant rate, remains intuitive, and pairs cleanly with civil time.

      1. Direct UTC Mapping

        Umi‑time degrees (UT°) are computed as:

        UT° = 360° × (UTC_seconds_of_day / 86400)

        Where:

        • UTC_seconds_of_day is the number of elapsed SI seconds since 00:00:00 UTC.
        • 86400 is the fixed number of SI seconds in a civil day.

        This mapping ensures:

        • Umi‑time degrees advance uniformly.
        • Umi‑seconds match SI seconds.
        • The slow hand rotates once every 24 hours at a constant speed.
        • Umi‑time remains simple, predictable, and universally understandable.

      2. Continuous Fractional Degrees

        Fractional degrees are preserved:

        UT° = UT° mod 360

        This ensures a continuous, unbroken rotational measure.

      3. Solar Geometry for Dial Orientation (Not Degree Rate)

        Dial orientation is determined by longitude only.

        Solar geometry determines sunrise/sunset placement and other solar‑day complications, but does not affect the rate at which Umi‑time degrees advance.

        This hybrid model provides:

        • solar‑accurate noon/midnight alignment (via longitude)
        • sunrise/sunset positioning (via solar geometry)
        • intuitive civil‑time pairing
        • constant rotational speed

    6. Umi‑Seconds

      Umi‑seconds are defined directly from SI seconds.

      1. Definition

        One Umi‑second is exactly one SI second.

        This ensures:

        • uniform progression
        • compatibility with civil time
        • intuitive mental mapping
        • stable animation and dial behavior

      2. Fractional Umi‑Seconds

        Fractional Umi‑seconds are computed as:

        UmiSecond = UTC_seconds_of_day mod 86400

        They must be displayed as a continuously increasing value, not a ticking integer.

    7. Dial Orientation

      The Umi‑time dial is solar‑referenced but longitude‑oriented. Solar geometry determines sunrise/sunset placement and other solar‑day features, but dial orientation is determined by longitude only.

      1. Fixed Reference Points

        The Umi‑time dial uses two fixed visual anchors:

        • Top of dial → solar noon
        • Bottom of dial → solar midnight

        These positions do not change.

      2. Variable Sunrise / Sunset Positions

        Sunrise and sunset positions vary with:

        • latitude
        • season
        • atmospheric refraction
        • equation of time
        • terrain (optional correction)

        They must be computed dynamically and placed on the dial according to their Umi‑time degree values.

      3. Dial Rotation

        The dial rotates to align with the observer’s longitude.
        The degree values remain universal; the dial orientation changes.

    8. Solar‑Geometry‑Based Complications

      Umi‑time supports optional solar‑day visualizations (“complications”) that enrich the watch face with local solar information.

      • The dial is the rotating portion of the watch face. It contains the Umi‑time degree markers only.
      • The solar ring is a fixed portion of the watch face that surrounds the dial. It contains the sunrise/sunset markers and the color gradients representing periods of the day.

      Dial orientation (rotation) is determined by longitude only.
      Solar geometry affects what is drawn on the solar ring, not how the dial rotates.

      1. Dial Rotation (Solar Noon & Midnight Anchors)

        Purpose:
        Align the dial so that solar noon and solar midnight appear at fixed positions relative to the solar ring.

        Inputs Used (Dial Only):

        • Longitude (λ) — determines the rotation of the dial so that:
          • Top of dial → solar noon
          • Bottom of dial → solar midnight

        Notes:

        • Dial orientation is determined by longitude only.
        • The solar ring remains fixed; the dial rotates beneath it.
        • No other solar geometry inputs affect dial rotation.

      2. Sunrise / Sunset Points (Solar Ring)

        Purpose:
        Place sunrise and sunset markers on the solar ring at their correct Umi‑time degree positions.

        Inputs Used (Solar Ring):

        • Latitude (φ)
        • Solar declination (δ)
        • Solar hour angle (H)
        • Equation of Time (EoT)
        • Julian date (JD)
        • Atmospheric refraction model (optional)
        • Terrain horizon model (optional)

        Notes:

        • These inputs determine where sunrise and sunset appear on the solar ring.
        • The dial’s degree markers rotate beneath the fixed sunrise/sunset markers.
        • Sunrise/sunset positions vary throughout the year and must be computed dynamically.
        • These calculations do not change dial orientation.

        1. Sky Gradients (Golden Hour, Blue Hour, Twilight) — Solar Ring

          Purpose:
          Render twilight phases and color gradients on the solar ring around sunrise and sunset.

          Inputs Used (Solar Ring):

          • Latitude (φ)
          • Solar declination (δ)
          • Solar altitude (α)
          • Solar azimuth (A)
          • Julian date (JD)
          • Atmospheric refraction model
          • Civil / nautical / astronomical twilight altitude thresholds

          Notes:

          • Twilight phases depend on solar altitude relative to the horizon.
          • Gradients are drawn on the solar ring; the dial remains purely degree markers.
          • These visual effects do not affect dial orientation or degree progression.

      3. Local Civil Time Indicator

        Purpose:
        Display local civil time in relation to Umi‑time, either on the dial or as an auxiliary marker on the solar ring.

        Inputs Used:

        • Timezone offset (UTC±X)
        • Daylight saving rules (if applicable)

        Notes:

        • Civil time is mapped onto Umi‑time degrees as an auxiliary indicator.
        • Timezone does not affect dial orientation.
        • This complication helps users relate Umi‑time degrees to familiar civil time without altering the dial’s rotation.

    9. Solar Event Computation Formulas

      Umi‑Time uses two angular systems:

      • UT° — Umi‑Time degrees (a time coordinate derived from UTC)
      • ° — physical angle on the solar ring (a spatial coordinate)

      The solar ring defines 0° at solar midnight, located at the bottom of the watch face.
      Angles increase clockwise.

      All solar events are computed in UTC, converted to UT°, then mapped to solar‑ring angles relative to solar midnight.

      1. Umi‑Time Degree Conversion (UT°)

        For any event time expressed in UTC hours:

        \[ \text{UT°} = \frac{\text{UTC hours}}{24} \times 360 \]

        Example:
        08:00 UTC → \( \frac{8}{24} \times 360 = 120\ \text{UT°} \)

      2. Local Solar Midnight

        Local solar midnight is the moment when the Sun reaches its lowest altitude for a given observer. In Umi‑Time, this event is expressed directly in degrees based on the observer’s longitude.

        Because observers west of the prime meridian experience solar midnight later than 00:00 UTC, the correct Umi‑Time degree for solar midnight is:

        \[ UT_{\text{mid}} = -\lambda \]

        Where:

        • \(\lambda\) = geographic longitude (positive east, negative west)

        This aligns solar midnight correctly on the Umi‑Time dial for all longitudes.

      3. Dial Rotation Angle (°)

        The dial rotates so that solar midnight appears at 0° (bottom of the solar ring).

        \[ \theta_{\text{rotation}} = -\text{UT°}_{\text{mid}} = \lambda \]

        This is a physical rotation in degrees.

        The solar ring does not rotate.

      4. Solar Ring Angle Mapping

        For any solar event with Umi‑Time degree \( \text{UT°}_{\text{event}} \):

        \[ \text{Angle}_{\text{event}} = \left( \text{UT°}_{\text{event}} - \text{UT°}_{\text{mid}} \right) \mod 360^\circ \]

        This yields the physical angle (°) on the solar ring.

      5. Core Solar Geometry

        This section defines all solar‑geometry quantities required for computing sunrise, sunset, and related solar events. All formulas are self‑contained and require only the observer’s location and the UTC date/time. Explanation of these quantities is beyond the scope of this specification, but references are provided for further study.

        1. Julian Date

          Determine number of days since the epoch J1900. Start by separating the date into year, month, day, hour, minutes and seconds components, combining the hours, minutes and seconds into a value representing a partial day.

          \[ Y = \text{year} \] \[ M = \text{month (1-12)} \] \[ D = \text{day (1-31)} \] \[ UT = \text{hour} + \frac{\text{minutes}}{60} + \frac{\text{seconds}}{3600} \]

          If the month is January or February, treat it as the 13th or 14th month of the previous year:

          \[ \text{if } M \leq 2, \text{ then } Y' = Y - 1, M' = M + 12 \text{, else } Y' = Y, M' = M \]

          Next, compute the factors A, B, C, E & F as follows:

          \[ A = \left\lfloor \frac{Y'}{100} \right\rfloor \] \[ B = 2 - A + \left\lfloor \frac{A}{4} \right\rfloor \] \[ C = \left\lfloor 365.25 (Y' + 4716) \right\rfloor \] \[ E = \left\lfloor 30.6001 (M' + 1) \right\rfloor \]

          Finally, compute the Julian Date:

          \[ JD = D + B + C + E - 1524.5 + \frac{UT}{24} \]

          For convenience, the Julian Century is:

          \[ T = \frac{JD - 2451545.0}{36525} \]

        2. Solar Mean Longitude

          \[ L_0 = 280.46646^\circ + 36000.76983^\circ T + 0.0003032^\circ T^2 \]

        3. Solar Mean Anomaly

          \[ M = 357.52911^\circ + 35999.05029^\circ T - 0.0001537^\circ T^2 \]

        4. Earth Orbit Eccentricity

          \[ e = 0.016708634 - 0.000042037 T - 0.0000001267 T^2 \]

        5. Sun’s Equation of Center

          \[ C = \sin(M) \cdot (1.914602 - T \cdot (0.004817 + 0.000014 \cdot T)) + \sin(2M) \cdot (0.019993 - 0.000101 \cdot T) + \sin(3M) \cdot 0.000289 \]

        6. Ecliptic Longitude of the Sun

          \[ \lambda_\odot = L_0 + C \]

        7. True Anomaly of the Sun

          \[ \nu = M + C \]

        8. Radius Vector of the Sun

          \[ r = \frac{1.000001018 \cdot (1 - e^2)}{1 + e \cdot \cos(\nu)} \]

        9. Apparent Longitude of the Sun

          \[ \lambda_{\text{sun}} = \lambda_\odot - 0.00569^\circ - 0.00478^\circ \cdot \sin(125.04^\circ - 1934.136^\circ T) \]

        10. Mean Obliquity of the Ecliptic

          \[ \epsilon_0 = 23 + \frac{26 + \frac{21.448 - T \cdot (46.815 + T \cdot (0.00059 - T \cdot 0.001813))}{60}}{60} \]

        11. Obliquity Correction

          \[ \epsilon = \epsilon_0 + 0.00256^\circ \cdot \cos(125.04^\circ - 1934.136^\circ T) \]

        12. Right Ascension of the Sun

          \[ \alpha = \arctan2\left( \cos(\epsilon) \cdot \sin(\lambda_{\text{sun}}), \cos(\lambda_{\text{sun}}) \right) \]

        13. Solar Declination

          \[ \delta = \arcsin\left( \sin\epsilon \cdot \sin\lambda_{\text{sun}} \right) \]

        14. Obliquity Coefficient

          \[ Y = \tan^2\left( \frac{\epsilon}{2} \right) \]

        15. Equation of Time

          The Equation of Time (EoT) expresses the difference between apparent solar time and mean solar time:

          \[ \text{EoT} = 4 \left( Y \sin(2L_0) - 2e \sin M + 4eY \sin M \cos(2L_0) - 0.5Y^2 \sin(4L_0) - 1.25 e^2 \sin(2M) \right) \]

      6. Solar Event Formulas (UT°)

        Each solar event is expressed in Umi‑Time degrees (UT°). Solar midnight is defined in Section 9.2 and is not recomputed here. Solar noon and sunrise/sunset are derived from that value.

        1. Sunrise / Sunset Hour Angle

          The sunrise/sunset hour angle \(H_0\) is computed using standard solar‑position equations:

          \[ H_0 = \arccos\left( \frac{-\sin\phi \cdot \sin\delta - \sin h_0} {\cos\phi \cdot \cos\delta} \right) \]

          Where:

          • \(\phi\) = latitude
          • \(\delta\) = solar declination
          • \(h_0\) = altitude threshold (≈ −0.833° including refraction)

          Note: The computation of the hour angle may fail when the sun is circumpolar (i.e., it never sets or never rises). This failure must be detected, and handled by skipping the sunrise/sunset calculations as appropriate.

        2. Solar Midnight (UT°)

          \[ UT_{\text{mid}} = \left( -\lambda - \frac{\text{EoT}}{4} \right) \bmod 360^\circ \]

        3. Solar Noon (UT°)

          \[ UT_{\text{noon}} = \left( 180^\circ - \lambda - \frac{\text{EoT}}{4} \right) \bmod 360^\circ \]

        4. Sunrise Time

          Sunrise occurs \(H_0\) degrees before solar noon:

          \[ UT_{\text{sunrise}} = \left( UT_{\text{noon}} - H_0 \right) \bmod 360^\circ \]

        5. Sunset Time

          Sunset occurs \(H_0\) degrees after solar noon:

          \[ UT_{\text{sunset}} = \left( UT_{\text{noon}} + H_0 \right) \bmod 360^\circ \]

        6. Daylight Length

          Daylight length is the difference between sunset and sunrise:

          \[ \text{Daylight Length} = UT_{\text{sunset}} - UT_{\text{sunrise}} \]

          Or twice the Hour Angle

          \[ \text{Daylight Length} = 2 H_0 \]

      7. Twilight Boundaries (UT°)

        Twilight boundaries use altitude thresholds:

        • Astronomical: \( h = -18^\circ \)
        • Nautical: \( h = -12^\circ \)
        • Civil: \( h = -6^\circ \)
        • Blue hour: \( h \approx -4^\circ \)
        • Golden hour: \( h \approx +6^\circ \)

        General hour‑angle formula:

        \[ H(h) = \arccos\left( \frac{\sin h - \sin\phi \cdot \sin\delta} {\cos\phi \cdot \cos\delta} \right) \]

        Morning event time:

        \[ t_{\text{event, AM}} = t_{\text{noon}} - \frac{H(h)}{15^\circ} \]

        Evening event time:

        \[ t_{\text{event, PM}} = t_{\text{noon}} + \frac{H(h)}{15^\circ} \]

        Convert each to UT°:

        \[ \text{UT°}_{\text{event}} = \frac{t_{\text{event}}}{24} \times 360 \]

        Note: The computation of the hour angle may fail when the sun is circumpolar (i.e., it never sets or never rises). This failure must be detected, and handled by skipping the associated twilight event calculations as appropriate.

      8. Solar Ring Angles for All Events

        After computing UT° for each event:

        \[ \text{Angle}_{\text{event}} = \left( \text{UT°}_{\text{event}} - \text{UT°}_{\text{mid}} \right) \mod 360^\circ \]

        This yields the physical angle on the solar ring for:

        • Solar midnight
        • Astronomical twilight (AM/PM)
        • Nautical twilight (AM/PM)
        • Civil twilight (AM/PM)
        • Blue hour (AM/PM)
        • Golden hour (AM/PM)
        • Sunrise
        • Sunset
        • Solar noon

        All drawn on the fixed solar ring, with the dial rotating beneath it.

    10. Accuracy Requirements

      1. Solar Position Accuracy

        Solar‑position accuracy affects the placement of sunrise, sunset, solar noon, and solar midnight on the Umi‑Time dial. It does not affect dial orientation or the progression of Umi‑Time degrees.

        Implementations should achieve:

        • ±0.1° solar azimuth accuracy
        • ±0.1° solar altitude accuracy
        • ±10 second sunrise/sunset accuracy

        These values are achievable using the self‑contained solar‑geometry formulas provided in this specification and are sufficient for correct visual placement of all solar‑day features on the Umi‑Time dial.

      2. Time Synchronization

        Civil time input should be synchronized to:

        • NTP
        • GPS
        • OS system clock

    11. Textual representation of Umi‑Time

      1. Scope

        This section specifies the textual representations of Umi‑Time for use in data interchange, documentation, and user interfaces. It defines the normative formats for expressing Umi‑Time as a standalone value, as well as its combination with a civil calendar date. The formats described herein include both basic and extended representations, optional local context suffixes, and rules for disambiguation when Umi‑Time and civil time may appear in the same context.

        The representations defined in this section are patterned after the conventions of ISO 8601 and are intended to be compatible with future extensions of that standard. These representations do not redefine civil time, do not modify the semantics of ISO 8601 date formats, and do not impose requirements on systems that do not implement Umi‑Time.

        This section does not specify computational methods for determining Umi‑Time, nor does it define algorithms for converting between Umi‑Time and civil time. Such methods are defined elsewhere in this specification. The present section concerns only the normative syntax, structure, and interpretation of Umi‑Time textual formats.

      2. Normative references

        The following referenced documents are indispensable for the application of this section. For dated references, only the edition cited applies. For undated references, the latest edition of the referenced document (including any amendments) applies.

        • ISO 8601‑1, Date and time — Representations for information interchange — Part 1: Basic rules
        • ISO 8601‑2, Date and time — Representations for information interchange — Part 2: Extensions
        • ISO 31‑1, Quantities and units — Part 1: Space and time
        • ISO/IEC 14977, Extended Backus–Naur Form (EBNF) — Syntax notation
        • IERS Conventions, International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service — Standards for Earth orientation parameters and time scales

        Informative references to astronomical models, solar ephemerides, or computational methods for determining Umi‑Time may be provided elsewhere in this specification, but they are not required for interpreting the textual formats defined in this section.

      3. Terms and definitions

        For the purposes of this section, the following terms and definitions apply. Terms defined elsewhere in this specification, or in ISO 8601, shall retain their established meanings unless explicitly redefined here.

        • Umi‑Time
          A representation of Earth’s rotational position expressed in UT degrees and UT seconds. Umi‑Time is a global, location‑independent time scale derived from the continuous rotation of Earth rather than from civil timekeeping conventions.
        • UT degree
          A unit representing one three‑hundred‑and‑sixtieth of Earth’s rotation. UT degrees are numbered from 000 to 359, increasing monotonically as Earth rotates eastward.
        • UT second
          A subdivision of a UT degree equal to 1/240 of that degree. UT seconds are numbered from 000 to 239. One UT second corresponds to one SI second multiplied by the ratio between the length of a solar day and 360×240.
        • Basic Umi‑Time format
          A fixed‑width textual representation of Umi‑Time consisting of a three‑digit UT degree field followed by a three‑digit UT second field, without separators.
        • Extended Umi‑Time format
          A textual representation of Umi‑Time in which the UT degree and UT second fields are separated by a colon (:), improving human readability while preserving fixed‑width fields.
        • U‑separator
          The literal character U used to separate a civil calendar date from a Umi‑Time value. The U‑separator distinguishes Umi‑Time from civil time in combined date–time representations.
        • Local context suffix
          An optional annotation appended to a Umi‑Time value that conveys location‑dependent or observer‑dependent information, such as longitude or segment‑of‑day classification. Local context suffixes do not alter the underlying Umi‑Time value.
        • Longitude offset suffix
          A local context suffix expressing the observer’s longitude in UT degrees, using a sign (+ east, - west) followed by a three‑digit degree value.
        • Segment code suffix
          A local context suffix identifying the observer’s position within a geometric subdivision of the local solar day. Segment codes are expressed as S<n>-<k>, where n is the number of segments and k is the zero‑based index of the segment. An optional refinement (+dd) may indicate degrees into the segment.
        • Shorthand Umi‑Time
          A six‑digit representation of Umi‑Time identical in width to the ISO 8601 basic civil time format (hhmmss). Shorthand Umi‑Time is context‑dependent and may require disambiguation.
        • Disambiguation
          The process of determining whether a six‑digit time value represents civil time or Umi‑Time. Disambiguation may rely on context, the presence of the U‑separator, or the use of extended Umi‑Time formats.

      4. General principles

        Umi‑Time is a representation of Earth’s rotational position expressed in UT degrees and UT seconds. A full rotation of Earth corresponds to 360 UT degrees, and each UT degree corresponds to 240 UT seconds. Umi‑Time is therefore a continuous, cyclic, and location‑independent time scale derived from the physical rotation of Earth rather than from civil timekeeping conventions.

        Textual representations of Umi‑Time shall use fixed‑width numeric fields and zero‑padding to ensure unambiguous interpretation. UT degrees shall be represented as a three‑digit integer in the range 000–359, and UT seconds shall be represented as a three‑digit integer in the range 000–239. These ranges and widths are normative and shall not be altered by implementations.

        Umi‑Time may be represented either as a standalone value or in combination with a civil calendar date. When combined with a date, Umi‑Time shall be separated from the date using the literal character U. The U‑separator distinguishes Umi‑Time from civil time in contexts where both may appear and ensures compatibility with ISO 8601 date formats.

        Local context information, such as the observer’s longitude or the observer’s position within a geometric subdivision of the local solar day, may be appended to a Umi‑Time value using optional suffixes. These suffixes are informative only and do not modify the underlying Umi‑Time value. A conforming implementation shall not rely on suffixes for determining the UT degree or UT second fields.

        In contexts where both civil time and Umi‑Time may be present, a conforming implementation shall apply the disambiguation rules defined in this section. In particular, the presence of the U‑separator or the use of the extended Umi‑Time format shall be used to distinguish Umi‑Time from civil time when ambiguity is possible.

        This section defines only the textual representation of Umi‑Time. The computational methods for determining Umi‑Time, converting between Umi‑Time and civil time, or deriving local context information are specified elsewhere in this document and are not part of the present section.

      5. Basic Umi‑Time format

        The basic Umi‑Time format is a fixed‑width numeric representation consisting of two fields: a three‑digit UT degree field followed immediately by a three‑digit UT second field. The basic format shall be written without separators and shall use zero‑padding for both fields to ensure unambiguous interpretation.

        UT degrees shall be represented as a three‑digit integer in the range 000–359, increasing monotonically with Earth’s rotation. UT seconds shall be represented as a three‑digit integer in the range 000–239, corresponding to subdivisions of a UT degree. Both fields are mandatory in the basic format.

        The basic Umi‑Time format is always six digits in length. This distinguishes it from the ISO 8601 basic civil time format, which consists of three two‑digit fields (hhmmss). A conforming implementation shall accept and produce the basic Umi‑Time format for machine‑oriented data interchange.

        Example:

        235198

        1. Field definitions

          The basic Umi‑Time format consists of two numeric fields: a UT degree field and a UT second field. Both fields shall be represented using fixed‑width, zero‑padded decimal integers to ensure unambiguous interpretation and compatibility with machine‑oriented data interchange.

          • UT degree field (ddd)
            A three‑digit integer in the range 000–359. The UT degree field represents Earth’s rotational position measured in UT degrees, increasing monotonically from 000 at the start of the UT day to 359 immediately before the next UT day begins.
          • UT second field (sss)
            A three‑digit integer in the range 000–239. The UT second field represents subdivisions of a UT degree, with each UT second equal to 1/240 of a UT degree. The UT second field shall always contain exactly three digits.

          Both fields are mandatory in the basic Umi‑Time format. No other fields, separators, or symbols shall appear in the basic representation. The concatenation of the UT degree and UT second fields yields a six‑digit Umi‑Time value.

        2. Basic format (UTD)

          The basic Umi‑Time format, designated UTD, shall consist of a three‑digit UT degree field immediately followed by a three‑digit UT second field, without separators or additional symbols. Both fields shall be represented using fixed‑width, zero‑padded decimal integers.

          The UT degree field shall contain a value in the range 000–359. The UT second field shall contain a value in the range 000–239. A conforming implementation shall not omit either field and shall not alter their widths or numeric ranges.

          The basic UTD format is always six digits in length. This fixed width distinguishes Umi‑Time from the ISO 8601 basic civil time format, which consists of three two‑digit fields (hhmmss). A conforming implementation shall accept and produce the basic UTD format for machine‑oriented data interchange.

          Example:

          235198

        3. Zero‑padding rules

          All numeric fields in the basic Umi‑Time format shall be represented using fixed‑width, zero‑padded decimal integers. Zero‑padding is mandatory and shall not be omitted. This requirement ensures that Umi‑Time values are unambiguous, machine‑readable, and compatible with fixed‑width parsing rules used in ISO 8601.

          The UT degree field shall always consist of exactly three digits. Values less than 100 shall be left‑padded with zeros. For example, UT degree 7 shall be represented as 007, and UT degree 42 shall be represented as 042.

          The UT second field shall also consist of exactly three digits. Values less than 100 shall be left‑padded with zeros. For example, UT second 3 shall be represented as 003, and UT second 58 shall be represented as 058.

          A conforming implementation shall not shorten, omit, or expand either field, and shall not use space‑padding or other padding characters. Zero‑padding applies equally to the basic UTD format and the extended format in which the fields are separated by a colon.

          Examples:

          007003
                    042:058
                    359239

        4. Comparison with civil time formats

          The basic Umi‑Time format (dddsss) differs structurally and semantically from the ISO 8601 basic civil time format (hhmmss). Civil time represents hours, minutes, and seconds using three two‑digit fields, whereas Umi‑Time represents UT degrees and UT seconds using two three‑digit fields. These differences ensure that the two formats are distinguishable in most contexts.

          Civil time fields (hh, mm, ss) each have a maximum value below 60, whereas Umi‑Time fields (ddd, sss) have maximum values of 359 and 239 respectively. A conforming implementation shall not interpret a six‑digit Umi‑Time value as civil time unless explicitly indicated by context.

          In contexts where both civil time and Umi‑Time may appear, the presence of the U‑separator (U) or the use of the extended Umi‑Time format (ddd:sss) shall be used to distinguish Umi‑Time from civil time. A conforming implementation shall apply these disambiguation rules whenever ambiguity is possible.

          Examples:

          
                    Civil time (ISO 8601):   235959   
                    Umi‑Time (UTD):          235198   
                    Umi‑Time (extended):     235:198
                    Date + Umi‑Time:         2026-08-24U235:198

      6. Extended Umi‑Time format

        The extended Umi‑Time format introduces a separator between the UT degree field and the UT second field to improve human readability while preserving fixed‑width numeric fields. In the extended format, UT degrees and UT seconds shall be separated by a colon (:), and both fields shall retain the same ranges and zero‑padding rules defined for the basic format.

        A conforming implementation shall represent UT degrees as a three‑digit integer in the range 000–359, followed by a colon, followed by UT seconds represented as a three‑digit integer in the range 000–239. No additional separators, spaces, or symbols shall appear within the extended format.

        The extended format is intended for human‑readable contexts and may be used wherever Umi‑Time is presented to users. It is also suitable for use in combined date–time representations when accompanied by the U‑separator.

        Example:

        235:198

        1. Separator rules

          In the extended Umi‑Time format, the UT degree field and the UT second field shall be separated by a single colon (:). The colon is the only permitted separator between these fields. No spaces, additional punctuation, or alternative characters shall be used in place of the colon.

          The separator shall appear exactly once and shall occur immediately after the UT degree field. The UT second field shall follow the separator without intervening whitespace. A conforming implementation shall reject any representation in which the separator is omitted, repeated, or replaced by another character.

          The use of the colon separator does not alter the fixed‑width requirements of the UT degree and UT second fields. Both fields shall retain their three‑digit, zero‑padded form regardless of the presence of the separator.

          The separator rules defined in this section apply only to the extended Umi‑Time format. The basic Umi‑Time format shall not include a separator, and the date–Umi‑Time combination shall use the U‑separator as defined in this specification.

          Example:

          235:198

        2. Degree‑symbol variant (informative)

          In addition to the normative extended Umi‑Time format, an alternative human‑readable variant may be used in documentation, educational material, or user interfaces. In this variant, the UT degree field is followed by the degree symbol (°) and a space, after which the UT second field is presented as a three‑digit, zero‑padded integer.

          The degree‑symbol variant is intended solely for human readability and shall not be used for machine‑oriented data interchange or in contexts requiring strict conformance with ISO 8601‑style syntax. This variant does not alter the numeric ranges or zero‑padding rules of the UT degree or UT second fields.

          A conforming implementation may display this variant to users, but shall not rely on it for parsing or validation. Systems that require unambiguous interpretation shall use either the basic UTD format or the extended format with a colon separator.

          Example (informative):

          235° 198

      7. Date–Umi‑Time combination

        A calendar date may be combined with a Umi‑Time value to form a single textual representation. To avoid ambiguity with ISO 8601 civil date–time formats, this specification defines a distinct separator, the literal character U, which shall be used exclusively to introduce a Umi‑Time component following a calendar date.

        The calendar date shall conform to ISO 8601 and may be expressed in either basic or extended form. When combined with Umi‑Time, the date shall be followed immediately by the U‑separator, after which the Umi‑Time value shall appear in either basic or extended format. No whitespace or additional punctuation shall appear between the date, the U‑separator, or the Umi‑Time component.

        The use of the U‑separator ensures that combined date–Umi‑Time representations remain distinguishable from civil date–time representations that use the T separator. A conforming implementation shall apply the U‑separator whenever a calendar date and a Umi‑Time value are presented together.

        Example:

        2026-08-24U235:198

        1. Use of the U‑separator

          When a calendar date is combined with a Umi‑Time value, the date and the Umi‑Time component shall be separated by the literal character U. The U‑separator is mandatory in all combined date–Umi‑Time representations and shall appear immediately after the calendar date, with no intervening whitespace.

          The U‑separator serves to distinguish Umi‑Time from civil time in contexts where both may appear. ISO 8601 civil date–time representations use the T separator; therefore, the use of U ensures that combined date–Umi‑Time values remain unambiguous and machine‑interpretable.

          A conforming implementation shall not substitute any other character for the U‑separator, nor shall it omit the separator when a calendar date and a Umi‑Time value are presented together. The U‑separator applies equally to both the basic and extended Umi‑Time formats.

          Example:

          2026-08-24U235:198

        2. Calendar date formats

          When a calendar date is combined with a Umi‑Time value, the date component shall conform to ISO 8601. A calendar date may be expressed in either basic format (YYYYMMDD) or extended format (YYYY‑MM‑DD). A conforming implementation shall accept both formats when used in conjunction with the U‑separator.

          The calendar date shall appear in its entirety before the U‑separator. No whitespace, punctuation, or additional symbols shall intervene between the date and the U‑separator. The U‑separator shall immediately follow the final digit of the date component.

          The use of ISO 8601 date formats ensures compatibility with existing civil timekeeping systems and allows combined date–Umi‑Time values to be interpreted consistently across implementations. This specification does not alter the semantics of ISO 8601 calendar dates.

          Examples:

          
                    Basic date + Umi‑Time:    20260824U235198
                    Extended date + Umi‑Time: 2026‑08‑24U235:198

        3. Combined representations

          A combined date–Umi‑Time representation shall consist of a valid ISO 8601 calendar date immediately followed by the U‑separator (U) and a Umi‑Time value in either basic or extended format. No whitespace, punctuation, or additional symbols shall appear between these components.

          The combined representation forms a single, unambiguous textual value that identifies both the civil calendar date and Earth’s rotational position at that date. The U‑separator ensures that the representation cannot be misinterpreted as an ISO 8601 civil date–time, which uses the T separator.

          A conforming implementation shall accept both basic and extended Umi‑Time formats when combined with a date. The choice of format does not affect the semantics of the combined representation. Implementations shall preserve the fixed‑width, zero‑padded fields of Umi‑Time regardless of the date format used.

          Combined representations may be used in data interchange, logging, time‑stamping, and any context where both civil date and Umi‑Time must be recorded together. This specification does not define computational relationships between the calendar date and the Umi‑Time value; the two components are independent.

          Examples:

                    2026‑08‑24U235:198

      8. Local context suffixes

A conforming implementation shall treat the suffix as informative metadata and shall ignore it when parsing or validating the underlying Umi‑Time value.       10. Parsing and validation rules

        The UT degree field shall be parsed as a three‑digit, zero‑padded integer in the range 000–359. The UT second field shall be parsed as a three‑digit, zero‑padded integer in the range 000–239. A conforming implementation shall reject any representation in which these fields fall outside their defined ranges or violate fixed‑width requirements.

        When the extended format is used, the colon separator (:) shall appear exactly once and shall separate the UT degree and UT second fields. When the basic format is used, no separators shall appear. A conforming implementation shall not attempt to infer the intended format from context; the representation itself shall determine validity.

        Optional components—including the U‑separator, date prefix, longitude offset suffix, segment code suffix, and degree‑offset refinement—shall be parsed only when present and shall be validated according to their respective syntactic rules.         A Umi‑Time value shall be considered invalid if any component violates its defined syntax, if fields are missing or malformed, or if the representation contains extraneous characters, whitespace, or punctuation not permitted by this specification.         3. Suffix validation

          A conforming implementation shall validate all suffixes appended to a Umi‑Time value according to the syntactic and numeric rules defined in this specification. Suffixes are strictly optional and shall not alter the interpretation of the UT degree or UT second fields. Any malformed or out‑of‑range suffix shall render the entire Umi‑Time value invalid.

          Each suffix category—longitude offset, segment code, and degree‑offset refinement—shall be validated independently. A conforming implementation shall not infer the meaning of a suffix from context, nor shall it attempt to reinterpret one suffix category as another. The leading symbol or letter of each suffix shall uniquely identify its category.

          Longitude offset suffixes shall consist of a leading plus or minus sign followed by a three‑digit, zero‑padded integer in the range 000–180. Segment code suffixes shall consist of an uppercase letter in the range A–Z followed by one or more decimal digits whose range is defined by the segment system in use. Degree‑offset refinements shall consist of a leading plus or minus sign followed by a one‑ or two‑digit integer in the range 0–99.

          A conforming implementation shall reject any suffix containing lowercase letters, non‑decimal digits, extraneous punctuation, incorrect zero‑padding, or characters not permitted by this specification. Suffixes shall appear immediately after the Umi‑Time value or after any preceding suffixes in the order defined by this specification. Whitespace shall not appear within or between suffixes.

          When multiple suffixes are permitted, each suffix shall be validated independently and the combined sequence shall be validated as a whole. A conforming implementation shall reject any representation in which suffixes appear in an incorrect order, overlap syntactically, or violate the rules governing their respective categories.

          Examples (informative):

                    Valid:   235:198S8
                    Valid:   235198S12-3
                    Invalid: 235198+181      
                    Invalid: 235198s8        
                    Invalid: 235198S09       
                    Invalid: 235198++45

    12. Conformance

      A conforming Umi‑Time implementation is any system, device, library, or application that correctly follows the definitions, formulas, and behavioral requirements specified in this document.

      Conformance requires that:

      • The implementation computes Umi‑Time degrees according to the formulas defined in this specification.
      • Solar events (solar midnight, solar noon, sunrise, and sunset), if implemented, are placed according to the solar‑geometry and hour‑angle calculations provided in this specification.
      • The implementation does not redefine Umi‑Time or present an incompatible time system as Umi‑Time.
      • Any extensions, optimizations, or alternative internal algorithms produce results consistent with the outputs defined in this specification.

      Conformance does not require any particular software license, programming language, implementation strategy, or internal representation. Implementations may be open‑source or proprietary, as long as they accurately implement the Umi‑Time standard.

    13. Licensing

      Umi‑time is an open standard.
      Implementations may use any license, open-source or proprietary, provided they accurately implement the standard and do not present Umi‑Time as a different or incompatible time system.

    14. Summary

      Umi‑time provides a universal, continuous, planetary measure of Earth’s rotation relative to the Sun. It restores the physical meaning of the day, complements civil time, and offers a foundation for a future global time standard.
