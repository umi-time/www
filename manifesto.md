# The Umi‑Time Manifesto
### A planetary time system for a species that has outgrown civil time

---

## 1. We live inside a broken clock

Everyone feels it.

Civil time is inconsistent, political, and increasingly disconnected from the world it claims to measure. Twice a year, millions of people are forced to change their clocks — not because the planet changed, but because governments did. Time zones zig‑zag across borders in ways that make no physical sense. Noon rarely means “the Sun is highest.” Midnight rarely means “the day has turned.”

People feel the absurdity, even if they don’t have the words for it.

Civil time is a patchwork of historical accidents, economic compromises, and political negotiations. It is a system built for railroads, legislatures, and commerce — not for human beings living on a rotating planet.

We deserve better.

---

## 2. The day is not a schedule — it is a rotation

Civil time treats the day as a grid of numbers.  
But the day is not a grid.  
The day is a **rotation**.

Every sunrise, every shadow, every moment of warmth or darkness is part of a single, continuous motion: Earth turning beneath the Sun.

Civil time hides this motion.  
Umi‑time reveals it.

Umi‑time is not a human invention layered on top of nature.  
It is nature, expressed numerically.

---

## 3. The Sun is the original clock

Before civilizations, before calendars, before hours and minutes, there was the Sun.

Every organism on Earth evolved under its rhythm.  
Every culture built its earliest timekeeping around its motion.  
Every human instinctively understands its position.

Umi‑time expresses the day as a full 360° rotational cycle mapped uniformly across the 24‑hour civil day. The dial orientation — solar noon at the top, solar midnight at the bottom, sunrise and sunset placed according to their true positions — reflects the actual solar geometry at your location.

On the Umi‑time dial:

- **Solar noon** is always shown at the **top** — the Sun’s highest point.  
- **Solar midnight** is always shown at the **bottom** — the Sun’s lowest point.  
- **Sunrise and sunset** move around the dial throughout the year, shifting with latitude, season, and the equation of time.

Umi‑time degrees advance uniformly with civil time, while longitude determines how the dial is oriented to match your local solar noon, midnight, sunrise, and sunset. The degree value is universal; the dial orientation is local.

Umi‑time degrees provide a universal, uniform measure of the day, while the dial maps those degrees to your local sky.

---

## 4. People already feel the contradictions of civil time

Ask anyone:

- “Do you like changing clocks twice a year?”  
- “Does noon feel like noon?”  
- “Does your timezone make sense?”  
- “Why does your clock say one thing while the Sun says another?”

People shrug, laugh, complain — but they accept it because they think there is no alternative.

Umi‑time is the alternative.

It gives people a way to express the intuition they’ve always had:  
that time should be tied to the planet, not to politics.

---

## 5. Civil time coordinates locally; Umi‑time coordinates globally

Civil time works well when everyone shares the same time zone.  
But the moment multiple time zones enter the conversation, civil time becomes a liability.

Scheduling across zones requires translating offsets, abbreviations, DST rules, and regional exceptions.  
People miss meetings not because they misunderstand time, but because civil time is fragmented.

Umi‑time is universal.

A meeting at **210° Umi‑time** is the same moment everywhere on Earth.  
It does not depend on borders, offsets, or seasonal clock changes.

Civil time is a local clock.  
Umi‑time is a planetary clock.

Together, they form a dual‑system future:  
civil time for local life, Umi‑time for global coordination.


---

## 6. Motion is meaning — and civil time has lost its motion

Traditional clocks once taught us to *see* time:

- the second hand sweeping  
- the minute hand drifting  
- the hour hand gliding  

But civil time has become digital, static, and abstract.  
It ticks, but it does not move.

Umi‑time restores motion.

The **slow hand** rotates once per day — the true motion of Earth.  
The **fast hand** rotates once per 240 seconds — the pulse of planetary rotation.  
The **fractional Umi‑seconds** flow continuously — the heartbeat of the world.

Civil time ticks.  
Umi‑time breathes.

Civil time fractures the world:

- borders

It is the same everywhere.  
It does not change with seasons.  
It does not change with laws.  
It does not change with borders.  
It does not change with politics.

It is the time of the planet, not the time of nations.
